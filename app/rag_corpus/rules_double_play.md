# Double Play Rule

## Definition

A double play is when the defensive team records two outs on the same play. Double plays are common in baseball and can end a half-inning or momentum shift dramatically.

## Types of Double Plays

### Force Double Play (Most Common)

- Runner must advance to the next base (due to batter putting ball in play)
- Fielder can record an out by touching the base with the ball or tagging the runner
- Example: Runner on first, ground ball hit, fielder steps on second for force out, throws to first for second out

### Tag Double Play

- Both outs recorded by tagging runners
- Example: Runner on first, ground ball to shortstop, shortstop tags runner going to second, then throws to first baseman who tags batter/runner going to first

### Unassisted Double Play

- One fielder records both outs without help
- Rare but possible: fielder catches line drive (out 1) then tags runner who left base early (out 2)

## Significance

Double plays are highly valued defensively as they quickly end offensive opportunities. A team's ability to "turn two" (complete a force double play, especially on a ground ball with runners in scoring position) is a key defensive metric.

Source: Official Baseball Rules, Rule 2.00 and Rules 5-6.
